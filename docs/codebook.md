Each review receives one of the nine labels defined in Table B.1. Coders read the whole review and answer the questions in Table B.2 in order, stopping at the first “yes”. Examples are IGAR reviews outside the coded samples, translated from Indonesian.

**Table B.1.** Labels, definitions, examples, and boundary rules.

| Label | Definition (must be present in the text) | Example | Not this label if |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coercion | Rejects or questions the obligation to use the app to obtain a service or good, or the policy itself | “Strange, buying fuel through an app.” | “Must” refers only to updating the app |
| Distrust | Accuses the agency or app of bad intent: fraud, corruption, profit-seeking, deceit | “A bad app made by these corrupt people” | “Fraud” is only an insult about a login error (technical failure) |
| Exclusion | Names groups who cannot comply for lack of a phone, data, signal, age, or digital skills | “What about people who cannot afford it and people who are not tech-savvy??” | The signal is full but the app still fails (technical failure) |
| Privacy concern | Worries that personal data are misused, leaked, monitored, or requested excessively | Illustrative only (such reviews were rare): “afraid my personal data will leak” | An ID number is mentioned only because verification failed (technical failure) |
| Exit or workaround | Stops using or circumvents the mandate: buying from retailers, using another account, uninstalling as protest, installing only to rate | “Installed only to give a one-star rating, uninstalled right after” | Reinstalling to fix an error (technical failure) |
| Burden | The app or procedure is cumbersome or costly in time and effort, without rejecting the obligation itself | “Instead of making things easier, it makes them harder” | The complaint is joined by a rejection of the obligation (coercion) |
| Technical failure | The app does not work: errors, crashes, one-time passwords not received, failed login or verification, endless loading | “The OTP never gets sent even though I want to verify” | The error is cited only as a reason to reject the mandate (coercion) |
| Acceptance | Accepts or praises the app or policy; feels helped | “Very helpful, speeds up and eases health services” | “Thank you” only closes a complaint (label the complaint) |
| Other | Unrelated to the app or policy, unreadable, spam, generic insults without a stated reason, or neutral questions | “Great, hope to win the 5-billion prize” | A short review with a clear meaning, such as “keeps crashing” (technical failure) |

**Table B.2.** Priority order.

| Step | Question | Label if yes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the review unrelated to the app or policy, unreadable, or too short to judge? | Other |
| 2 | Does it reject or question the obligation or the policy? | Coercion |
| 3 | Does it accuse the agency of bad intent? | Distrust |
| 4 | Does it name a group that cannot comply? | Exclusion |
| 5 | Does it express concern about personal data? | Privacy concern |
| 6 | Does it state that the writer stops using or circumvents the mandate? | Exit or workaround |
| 7 | Does it complain that the app or procedure is cumbersome, without naming a specific fault? | Burden |
| 8 | Does it report that the app does not work? | Technical failure |
| 9 | Does it accept or praise the app or policy? | Acceptance |

For Proposition 3, coercion, burden, and distrust form the policy grievance group; technical failure forms its own group; all other labels form the third group. One-word approvals such as “ok” are coded as acceptance (Section 4.6).
